# ZONARYOS — PROJECT VISION AND SCOPE DOCUMENT

> **Status:** Start of the design/planning phase (no code written yet)
> **Purpose:** This document exists to fully capture the vision and scope before coding begins. It will grow, get clarified, and get detailed over the 1–2 month design/planning process.
> **Rule:** Only decisions the Developer has finalized are written here. No detail is skipped, but item headings stay short — so the same topic doesn't need to be re-thought during development.
> **Brand name finalized: ZonaryOS.** "Zonary" comes from the Latin root "zona" (belt/zone/region) in English; in biology, a "zonary placenta" is connective tissue that wraps around an organism like a belt. Adopted brand meaning: ZonaryOS is the core that wraps every area of a business (accounting, inventory, production, customer relations — nothing left outside) like a single belt.

---

## 1. CORE VISION (CLEAR DEFINITION)

ZonaryOS's reason for existing, in one sentence:

> **Any commercial business anywhere in the world — regardless of sector or scale — should be able to sign up on its own, with no outside help (consultant, sales rep, developer), and solve its entire end-to-end operational needs.**

### Key clarifications (points settled during discussion):

- **This is not an "against X" project.** Not positioned against third-party software, not against the consulting model. No defined competitor; the starting point is directly the **business + software** relationship itself.
- **Sector/scale independence must be absolute.** Whether it's a corner store or an aircraft factory, the same core must be able to meet the full needs of both. There will be no static, pre-coded structures in the system like a "Corner Store Module" or an "Aircraft Manufacturing Template."
- **"End-to-end" scope is not limited to the INSIDE of the business — it also covers the OUTSIDE.** That is:
  - Internal operations: accounting, inventory, production, logistics, WMS/MES, supply chain.
  - External relationships: **the business's relationship with its own customers is also part of the core** — CRM, order/demand management, customer communication, the quote-contract-invoice-collection cycle, a customer portal, and similar areas are all in scope. **Finalized: all of these are included** — CRM, B2B order synchronization, marketing/sales funnel, customer communication — every one of them is in scope, without exception, because ZonaryOS claims to be an end-to-end solution; no scope-narrowing choice will be made.
  - Potential implication: if a business and its supplier/customer are both in the same ecosystem (ZonaryOS), the relationship between them may not be an external integration but a natural extension of the system. *(This point hasn't been discussed in depth yet, to be detailed later.)*
- **The claim of "100% parametric without touching a line of code" is central.** Users must be able to build their own processes on their own. (Numbers like the wizard's step count are arbitrary placeholders, not yet a design detail — not something to get stuck on.)

### Test criterion (non-negotiable):
Any business owner signs up with no outside help, defines their own business, and the system meets that business's entire end-to-end needs (both internally and in its relationship with customers).

### No scope restriction (including MVP):
There will be no restriction to any particular country or sector. Therefore, from the very start of development, the system will be built with **infrastructure ready for any scenario** — it will not start with assumptions specific to a particular sector or country. (This decision closes the earlier open points about "MVP scope" and "first market/pilot region" in terms of scope restriction; however, how the practical development order/prioritization will work is still a separate matter — see Open Points.)

## 2. CORE PHILOSOPHY AND STARTING POINT (original document text)

The current ERP, WMS, and MRP software industry is locked in by clunky architectures, exorbitant licensing costs, and inflexible consulting processes. ZonaryOS is designed as a "Universal System Kernel" that any business, of any scale and sector, from a corner store to the aerospace industry, can use to manage its entire end-to-end operations from a single center, building its own processes 100% parametrically without touching a line of code.

The software will exist on a 100% open-source philosophy; but it will be democratized through a global cloud (SaaS) ecosystem that offers companies infrastructure management, cybersecurity, high performance, and a zero-installation barrier.

*(Note: The "consulting/competitor" emphasis in this section was part of the original draft; as clarified during discussion, the actual focus isn't competitive positioning but defining a universal solution. This section is kept for reference.)*

## 3. SYSTEM SCOPE AND CAPABILITIES

- **End-to-End Universal Management:** Accounting (A to Z), finance, inventory control, warehouse management (WMS), order management (OMS), logistics, a production-station tracking layer (MES), and supply chain all unify at a single database root. *(+ customer relationship layer — see §1)*
- **Full Sector Independence:** The system contains no static, pre-coded structures like a "Corner Store Module" or an "Aircraft Manufacturing Template." The system has a parametric DNA flexible enough to take the mathematical and operational shape of any business that enters it.
- **Self-Configuring Infrastructure:** With no need for a sales rep, consultant, or developer, the user completes a smart, dynamic wizard the first time they open the system. At the end of this wizard, the system automatically generates the business's workflow, form fields, and tables in the background.
  - **Wizard mechanism (finalized):** The wizard is not a flat/linear list of questions — it works as a **decision tree**. Every feature in ZonaryOS appears as a question on the appropriate branch of the tree. The tree branches based on the user's answers; irrelevant branches are never asked. **Every answer also opens the follow-up sub-questions that come after it** (a recursive, nested structure) — for example, the answer to a root question like "do you manufacture?" or "do you track inventory?" triggers new sub-questions specific to that branch. This way, the user builds a structure that exactly matches their business, step by step, through successive choices. At the end of the process, customer-specific screens and a **personalized structure** that delivers exactly the solution the business needs become active. (The "do you manufacture / do you track inventory" questions given are examples — the tree's full list of root branches hasn't been designed yet, see Open Points item 12.)
  - **AI Support Layer (finalized):** The decision-tree wizard remains the **permanent, foundational mechanism** — it is not replaced by an AI. AI is a **supportive layer added on top of it**, and stays accessible not just during setup but **throughout usage, in every area of the system, after setup as well**. **Its role should be thought of as a "knowledge base":** whenever anyone using ZonaryOS asks "how do I do this / how do I use this," the AI teaches them — it's a live, conversationally accessible knowledge source about the system's documentation and behavior. Purpose: since learning such a large, fully parametric system is naturally difficult, the user talks to the AI to quickly find answers to "how do I configure this for my business / where do I find this" and learns the system that way. **Critical boundary:** the AI never makes a change in the system on its own — every suggestion must go through **user approval** before being applied. *(Note: this is a different rule from the "Autonomous Decision Layer" in the Workflow Engine below — there, business rules can run fully autonomously if the customer chooses; here, the AI support layer always waits for approval, without exception. These two layers must not be confused with each other.)*
  - **Support Model (finalized):** The principle of "self-setup with no outside help" is met **through the AI Support Layer**, regardless of business scale (small corner store or large factory alike) — a separate, paid human support/consulting layer is **not being considered for now**. AI is designed as ZonaryOS's own support system.
- **Unlimited Process Flexibility (Workflow Engine):** A business's operating processes are managed not by code but by a graph-based state machine. For a corner store, the process turns into a single-step interface like "Stock In -> Point-of-Sale Checkout"; for an aircraft factory, the same system turns into a massive industrial tracking screen with steps like "Stock In -> Quality Control Lab -> Subcontracted Process -> Heat Treatment -> Assembly Station -> Final Test -> Shipping."
  - **Autonomous Decision Layer (finalized):** A goal like "dark factory" full autonomous management is not a fixed feature set — **the ability to make autonomous decisions must itself be parametric**. A customer can attach unlimited-depth sub-rules of the form **"if this condition occurs → automatically do this action"** to any workflow step in the wizard (e.g. "if stock of X drops below a level → automatically create a purchase order," "if sensor temperature exceeds Y → automatically stop the line"). Alongside each rule, the customer also chooses **whether this decision runs fully autonomously or waits for human approval** — the trust boundary is not fixed system-wide, it's a threshold the customer sets per rule. The mechanism is built entirely on a deterministic rule engine (no hidden/black-box ML layer needed) — unlimited-depth parametric sub-rule support structurally prevents any "this feature doesn't exist" situation. This is fully consistent with the system's general principle (nothing is hardcoded) and with the preference in item 6 (no AI needed in the Importer).
- **Universal Data Import (Universal Importer):** To eliminate the fear of switching software, businesses can upload data from their old systems (third-party ERP/accounting software, Excel, etc.) exactly as they exported it (export), directly into ZonaryOS in its raw form. The system does not impose fixed templates; it reads the raw data, dynamically expands the schema, and imports old data with a single click.
  - **Mechanism (finalized):** The import method varies based on the format of the exported data (JSON, CSV, Excel, etc. — the user chooses the method). The system shows the user a **draft mapping** of which columns exist in the source data and how they correspond to fields in ZonaryOS. If everything is clear, the user approves this draft and the data is imported with that mapping. **No AI is needed in this core flow** — it's a fully deterministic, user-approved process.
  - **Ready-Made Templates and AI's Role (finalized):** Over time (through community contributions), **ready-made templates** may emerge for known source systems — with a few questions like which system, which version, which menu it was exported from, ZonaryOS finds the right template and imports accordingly. Here too, AI's role is not to build/map the template itself, but to **guide** the user through finding the right template — this is fully consistent with the system-wide principle of "AI guides, the user/a parametric mechanism does the work."
- **Data Export Strategy (finalized):** As a counterpart to making import easy, export is also completely unrestricted — no vendor lock-in is created. The firm owner can export their own data **without limits** (CSV, JSON, Excel — no format restriction). For sub-roles, this permission depends on the firm owner's discretion via the existing **Parametric Permission/Role System** (the owner can grant export permission to a role, or not). The **shape, columns, and what's shown** in the exported data can be customized by the user from their own panel — the goal here too is **unlimited flexibility**.
- **Hardware and Machine Integration (Zero-Config Edge):** The barrier of a cloud architecture accessing devices on local networks is broken by a very lightweight, zero-dependency agent software (Edge Agent) running locally. This agent opens a secure, bidirectional tunnel to the cloud root without needing to open any external port. From the barcode printer and scale at a corner store to the CNC machines, industrial equipment, and sensors (OPC-UA, MQTT, Modbus) at a factory, every piece of hardware is instantly controlled by the system as if it were a local device.
  - **Bidirectional Bridge (finalized):** The Edge Agent doesn't just collect data (machine/sensor data, inbound) — it also **sends commands** (outbound) — for example, when a printout/invoice is requested from a terminal in ZonaryOS, the command goes to the local printer through the Edge Agent, not from the browser.
  - **Login and Identity (finalized):** The Edge Agent is a single installer file (not a firm-specific build). On first run, login happens with a **firm-specific Edge Agent account** (not a personal employee account). If there are installations across multiple locations/PCs, **each installation uses its own separate account** — so they can be revoked individually, without affecting each other, from the "Connected Devices" screen.
  - **Integrity Verification (finalized):** This is the Edge Agent-specific application of the same mechanism as the license verification described under the License section below — the agent verifies its file integrity and account validity with the central server on launch and periodically; if either check fails, it refuses to run.
  - **Revocability (finalized):** The firm's panel has a **"Connected Devices"** screen — every Edge Agent installation (location/device) is listed here and can be revoked individually. If a device's security is in question or it's no longer in use, the firm can cut off its access instantly.
  - **Permission System Integration (finalized):** The Edge Agent's capabilities (e.g. "can only send commands to the printer, cannot access accounting data") are determined through the system's general **Parametric Permission/Role System** — since the Edge Agent logs in with a firm account, it doesn't automatically get access to everything; it has its own role/permission scope.
  - **Updates (finalized):** The Edge Agent **updates automatically**, in a way that doesn't break existing operation and doesn't require manual intervention.
- **Parametric Permission/Role System (finalized):** Every button, every control, every form is **entirely controlled by permission parameters**. Firms can create their own roles and grant those roles permissions (which screen they can see, which button they can click, which form they can fill) according to their own needs.
  - **"Permission Audit Mode" mechanism (finalized):** Role/permission management doesn't happen in a separate "admin panel" — it happens **on the actual live screens the user already uses**. Every permission-gated button/link carries an invisible "this action requires this permission" tag in the background. When an authorized user turns on "Permission Audit Mode": (1) the system fetches the entire current role-permission table, (2) it stamps a badge (✓/×) next to every tagged button/link on the page, showing whether the currently logged-in user has that permission. Clicking the badge opens a popup that lists: the roles that have this permission (each with a "remove" option), and a dropdown to select and "add" permission to a role that doesn't have it yet, instantly. The change is saved **immediately**, badges and any open popups refresh automatically, active sessions for the affected role are reconnected so the new permissions take effect right away, and other admin screens are notified of the change live. Roles at the highest permission tier (that already have everything) don't appear in these lists. Buttons without a permission tag are flagged with a separate warning badge ("this button isn't wired to any permission check — it may have been forgotten") — a self-audit mechanism for catching permission checks forgotten during development.
  - **Auditor Role (finalized):** When a firm goes through an external audit process, it can use its own permission system (via the "Permission Audit Mode" mechanism above) to create a **limited-scope role specific to the auditor** and let the auditor log in with that role, under the auditor's own account — this is a natural extension of the existing parametric permission system, requiring no separate mechanism.
- **Audit Trail Infrastructure (partially finalized):** Every operation in the system will be traceable at the **most detailed level** — not just data changes (who/when/what changed), but **view/read access will also be logged**. These records are viewable by the firm administrator by default; external auditor access is provided via the "Auditor Role" above. **Retention period:** audit data will not be deleted — at least 10 years, likely longer; whether the exact duration is set as ZonaryOS's own (parametric) setting hasn't been finalized yet (see Open Points item 33). **Open legal question:** whether this level of detailed monitoring (particularly retaining employee view records) creates an issue under Turkey's data protection law (KVKK) hasn't been settled — Claude is not a lawyer and cannot give definitive legal advice on this; legal counsel is recommended before finalizing the design.
  - **Free-Form UI Design Mode (finalized):** The same pattern (switching to a special mode on the live page via a button) **also applies to UI design**. Organizations must be able to design their own layouts — the **position, shape, and size** of every piece of data shown on screen must be fully editable; this will be designed as a **fully flexible** structure. Design can be done both **manually (drag-and-drop)** and **with AI assistance** — but here too, AI's role is **not to do the design itself**, but to answer the user's "how should I design this?" question by guiding them. The actual design action is still carried out by the user — this is fully consistent with the general rule in the AI Support Layer above.
- **Unlimited/Parametric Compliance Framework (finalized):** ZonaryOS will be built so it can comply with **every audit/certification standard** that exists worldwide (ISO, GMP, Halal certification, etc.) or that emerges in the future. This is not a fixed/closed list — just like everything else (data schema, workflow, permission system), **compliance requirements are also parametric**, so that even a standard from a certifying body not known today can be added to the system later without touching code.
- **Corporate Certification (finalized):** Beyond the system complying with these standards, **ZonaryOS itself** (as an organization) will actually obtain the relevant certifications (including information-security certifications like ISO 27001, SOC 2).

## 4. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE GUIDE

> The decisions below were evaluated and finalized based on the three criteria the Developer set: security, stability, and performance.

- **Database Layer:** **PostgreSQL + JSONB** hybrid model — unifies relational data (accounting, receivables, finance) with the unpredictable dynamic data fields brought by different sectors, at a single root, without performance loss. Mature, proven, long security track record.
- **Time-Series Data (Machine Logs):** **TimescaleDB** — since it's a PostgreSQL extension, it doesn't require managing a separate database engine, and significantly boosts machine/sensor log performance without added operational load.
- **Multi-Tenancy Isolation:** **PostgreSQL Row-Level Security (RLS)** — isolation is guaranteed at the database engine level. Relying solely on application-code `tenant_id` filtering (which carries the risk of mixing all firms' data in a code bug) was judged an unacceptable risk for an ERP/accounting system, so RLS is mandatory.
- **Backend Services — Language:** **Go (Golang).** Runs with far fewer resources than Node.js under high concurrency; at the targeted scale (hundreds of firms, thousands of machines, millions of signal events in the first phase), Go's concurrency model (goroutines) is specifically designed for exactly this kind of high-volume, highly concurrent-connection workload — it can handle this scale. Sharing the same language with the Edge Agent (single toolchain) reduces maintenance burden; it has a large, accessible open-source contributor pool (aligned with the "thousands of contributors" goal).
- **Backend Services — Architecture:** **Modular Monolith** (NOT microservices). A structure with clear internal boundaries (each module has its own package/interface) but shipped as a single binary/single deployment. Fewer moving parts = fewer failure points (stability); development speed is preserved without the network latency, distributed tracing, and orchestration overhead that microservices bring. If genuinely needed later (a specific module needs to scale separately), the clear boundaries make it easy to split that module out into its own service.
- **Frontend:** **Next.js (React) & Tailwind CSS** — a single universal interface accessible directly from the browser with no local installation, dynamically simplifying or getting more detailed based on the business's scale.
- **Edge Agent Language:** **Go** — a zero-dependency single binary, easy cross-compilation to Windows/Linux/ARM, a strong concurrency model for managing multiple protocols (OPC-UA/MQTT/Modbus) simultaneously.
- **Message Queue:** **NATS JetStream** — lightweight, a single small binary, low operational overhead. Critical advantage: thanks to NATS's "leaf node" capability, **an embedded NATS instance can run inside the Edge Agent itself** — when the internet drops, the Edge Agent buffers data locally and auto-syncs once the connection returns. This meets the offline-resilience requirement (see Open Points item 29) with the same technology, without needing a separate solution.
- **Authentication:** **Keycloak** (self-hosted open-source IdP) — implements the OAuth2/OIDC standard, provides full control, doesn't create external data dependency. Judged to have no serious alternative.
- **Secrets Management:** **HashiCorp Vault, on self-hosted infrastructure.** A managed KMS service from any cloud provider (e.g. Oracle Cloud Vault) was **deliberately not chosen** — because the hardware will be set up in-house and the priority is avoiding external dependency/vendor lock-in, self-hosted Vault was preferred despite the added operational overhead, because it provides full control.
- **Multi-Language Support (i18n):** The entire infrastructure will be built with **multi-language support from the start**. No UI text will be hardcoded into the code; i18n practices/libraries will be used. This principle covers UI text — country-specific fiscal/tax compliance (e-invoice, e-ledger, etc.) is a separate matter and still open (see Open Points item 24).
- **Multi-Region / Uptime Strategy:** **Active-Passive, with fast automatic failover.** Mechanism: all write operations go to a **single global primary server**, regardless of the user's geographic location (e.g. Istanbul). Servers in at least 2 other locations (e.g. Tokyo, US) act as continuously updated **read-only replicas** — read requests from users in that region are served quickly from the local replica. If the primary server goes down, a healthy replica is automatically promoted to become the new primary (failover). **Known latency effect:** write operations from a region geographically far from the primary (e.g. Tokyo to Istanbul) carry added latency. This is largely offset by the NATS JetStream + Edge Agent architecture (above) — real-time factory operations (CNC commands, sensor reads) already happen locally, instantly, through the Edge Agent; the data going to the cloud is only for record-keeping/synchronization and is latency-tolerant. True active-active (multi-region simultaneous writes, e.g. CockroachDB/YugabyteDB) was evaluated but the added complexity/learning-curve cost wasn't judged necessary at this stage; a firm's data is generally written from its own region, so the need is low.
- **Multi-Tenancy — Shared Physical Infrastructure:** Even for large customers (e.g. an aircraft factory), a dedicated/separate physical server or database will **not** be offered — everyone shares the same physical infrastructure, with isolation handled logically (via RLS). Rationale: lighter control and maintenance overhead.

> **Note:** The reality of scale (starting solo/with a small team) and the priority of "not being externally dependent" were the basis for these decisions — options that bring heavy operational overhead OR create dependency on an external provider (microservices, true active-active DB, a managed cloud KMS) were deliberately ruled out.

## 5. DEVELOPMENT MODEL, LICENSE, AND ACCESS PRINCIPLES

- **Open-source development model:** The codebase will be developed **in a public GitHub repository from day one** — there is no closed/private phase. Unlimited contributors (potentially thousands of developers) can contribute. For now, **merge authority on the master branch belongs solely to the Developer** — a single-approver (BDFL-style) governance model. This isn't a permanent/unchangeable rule: if the project grows, merge authority may be delegated to trusted people in the future (a "core team" may form) — but for now, the sole decision-maker is the Developer.
- **Access model — there is NO self-hosting:** The software will exist under an open-source philosophy (the code is visible/inspectable) but **cannot be copied and run independently on a commercial basis**. The system will only be usable **through ZonaryOS's own central infrastructure**. This closes a previously open question (the possibility of a user setting up and running their own server) — no such option **exists**.
- **Database responsibility:** As a natural consequence, all customer data is kept on ZonaryOS's own infrastructure/database; no server/database management burden falls on businesses or organizations.
- **License:** A license will be applied to prevent commercial use/copying. The Developer's assessment: since this is an open-source project, no authorization/permission from an official body is needed — what's needed is a **corporate license agreement** that creates legal liability in the event of copying. *(Note: this is generally a correct approach — publishing an open-source license doesn't require government approval, it's a matter of private contract law. However, for the agreement to actually be binding and enforceable, it needs to be drafted/reviewed by a legal professional — Claude is not a lawyer and cannot give definitive legal advice on this, see Open Points item 20.)*
- **License verification approach (finalized):** The source code will be fully visible to everyone (the code will not be hidden/obfuscated), and in theory anyone could compile and run it on their own server — this **cannot be physically prevented**. However, the software **will not function** without connecting to ZonaryOS's central server and obtaining a valid license verification — meaning the code is technically runnable but not functional. This verification does more than check one thing: it also determines **which modules are active** on that installation (the license = both a validity check and a module-activation key). The mechanism's technical details (verification trigger frequency, offline behavior, etc.) haven't been finalized yet (see Open Points — item 32).
- **3-Layer Defense Against Commercial Copying (finalized):** In an open-source codebase, a technical guarantee that "no one can copy and run it" cannot be given (since the license check lives in the code itself, someone with enough knowledge could find and disable it). Despite this reality, a three-layer structure makes commercial copying practically pointless/risky:
  1. **Legal layer (the real deterrent):** the license agreement (see item 20) makes bypassing the code and using it commercially a legal violation — no one takes that risk at a serious/corporate scale.
  2. **Technical friction layer:** designing the license verification mechanism (item 32) with a certain amount of complexity makes bypassing it cumbersome for an ordinary user — not a complete barrier, but a deterrent.
  3. **Ecosystem dependency layer (the strongest practical deterrent):** a bypassed copy stays **cut off** from ZonaryOS's living ecosystem (multi-region redundancy/automatic recovery, the AI Support Layer, community-produced ready-made import templates, centralized security updates, the Edge Agent's own independent verification — hardware integration doesn't work on a bypassed copy — and ZonaryOS's corporate certification umbrella). Result: even if it technically runs, the user is cut off from most of the system's actual value — this removes the appeal of bypassing it in practice.
  - **Realistic goal:** not "no one can run it," but that **"running it is legally risky, technically cumbersome, and practically worthless."**
- **General technical quality principle:** All technical infrastructure — software, hardware, communication, security — will be built to **world standards and with current/state-of-the-art technology**. This is a binding principle for concrete technology choices made in the future (including the architecture in item 4); specific technology decisions will be evaluated against this principle.
- **Brand / White-Label:** This is a goal (not just an example). An organization/institution using ZonaryOS can use **its own name, logo, and domain** — the interface shown to its customers is presented under that organization's own brand. However, a **"ZonaryOS" attribution remains visible at the bottom of every page** (in a "Powered by ZonaryOS" style). Even though the visible brand belongs to the organization, **the entire infrastructure belongs to ZonaryOS** — this is fully consistent with the principle in item 5 (no self-hosting, all data/infrastructure centralized).
- **Domain/Subdomain Option:** During setup, a firm chooses one of two options: (1) use its own domain (e.g. `accounting.firm.com`) or (2) use a ZonaryOS subdomain (e.g. `firm.zonaryos.com`). For firms choosing their own domain, the required DNS routing and ZonaryOS-side technical configuration are done **automatically after setup** — the firm doesn't need to perform any manual technical steps.
- **Integrated Email Service:** ZonaryOS **also provides an email service** for firms without their own domain. Firms with their own email server/domain can connect it if they wish — in that case they can view and use email on their own domain **directly from within ZonaryOS**. The general principle here: **a firm using ZonaryOS shouldn't need any extra/external application for any of its work** — this principle isn't just about email, it's a general extension of the "end-to-end, single center" philosophy (see §1).

## 6. PRICING MODEL

> Criteria set by the Developer: transaction volume (B), number of connected devices (C), data/storage volume (D), and number of active modules (E) — seat/user count (A) was deliberately left out.

A three-tier model has been finalized:

- **Tier 1 — Module Base Fee (E):** A base subscription fee based on the modules activated in the wizard. A business that activates few modules (e.g. just basic accounting+inventory) pays a low base fee; a business that activates many modules (e.g. full production/MES tracking) pays a high base fee.
- **Tier 2 — Usage Band (B + D combined):** Since transaction volume and data/storage volume are naturally proportional to each other, they're combined under a single band system. Instead of paying per unit, the customer pays for whichever **pre-defined band** (e.g. "0–10,000 transactions/month + 5GB data") matches their usage intensity. This ensures predictability — reducing the risk of a surprise/variable bill.
- **Tier 3 — Per-Device Add-On Fee (C):** A simple, fixed add-on fee for every connected Edge Agent/device (printer, sensor, terminal connection, etc.).

**Total bill = Tier 1 (base) + Tier 2 (band) + Tier 3 (device count × unit fee).**

Concrete figures such as band thresholds, unit prices, and the module-to-base-fee mapping haven't been determined yet — this will be addressed separately as the product's scope becomes clearer (particularly alongside the development roadmap in item 8).
